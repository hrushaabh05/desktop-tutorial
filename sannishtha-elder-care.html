How it works

Three steps from worry to a written plan

Most families in India start planning on the day of a fall, a stroke or a hospital discharge. Starting even two years earlier changes what is possible.

1

Tell us about your parent

A 15-minute questionnaire on health, daily routines, home, family support and savings. Your parent can answer in their own language, or a family member can answer for them.

2

Get a ten-year care forecast

We model likely care stages using geriatric assessment tools such as the Katz ADL index, then price each stage with current rates for attendants, nursing and senior living in your city.

3

Act on it together

A care manager walks the family through the plan on one video call, sets up insurance and scheme enrolments, and shares a single dashboard so siblings in Pune, Dubai and Toronto see the same picture.

    Schemes, tax & legal

    Money families leave on the table

    Most of the families we meet are eligible for at least three of these and use one. We check eligibility and handle the paperwork.

    Health cover · age 70+₹5 lakh a year

    Ayushman Vay Vandana card

    Under AB PM-JAY every Indian aged 70 or older gets hospital cover regardless of income, on top of any existing family cover.

    Income · age 60+Up to ₹30 lakh

    Senior Citizens’ Savings Scheme

    Government-backed deposit paying quarterly interest at a rate set each quarter. A steady base for care expenses.

    Tax · Section 80D₹50,000 deduction

    Health insurance for parents

    Premiums or preventive check-ups paid for senior citizen parents are deductible. Uninsured seniors can claim medical bills instead.

    Tax · Section 80DDB₹1 lakh deduction

    Specified illnesses

    Treatment for conditions such as dementia, Parkinson’s and cancer for a senior citizen dependant.

    LegalAdvance Medical Directive

    A living will

    Since the Supreme Court’s 2018 ruling, simplified in 2023, your parent can record their wishes for end-of-life treatment. Few families know this is possible.

    Legal · 2007 ActMaintenance Tribunal

    Senior Citizens Act

    The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act protects a parent’s right to maintenance and to their property.

    From our gerontology team

    In Mumbai we see the same story every week. A parent in their late seventies, a third-floor flat with no lift, a slippery bathroom in the monsoon. Then a fall, a hip fracture, and a family making ten big decisions in one week in a hospital corridor.

    Almost every one of those decisions could have been made calmly, two years earlier, with the parent at the table. That is why Sannishtha Foundation started this programme. Ageing well at home is possible for most Indian elders. It just needs a plan.

    Questions families ask

    Frequently asked

    My parent is healthy. Isn’t it too early?

    This is the best time. Healthy parents can choose for themselves, qualify for insurance at better rates, and make changes to the home before a fall forces them. A plan made at 70 is mostly about prevention.

    Is a senior living community abandoning our parents?

    No. Many elders choose it for company, safety and a lift. We help families talk about this openly and visit communities together. For most families the plan is ageing at home with the right support, and we say so when that is the better option.

    How do you find trustworthy attendants?

    We work only with agencies that provide police verification, written contracts, replacement guarantees and basic GDA training. Our care manager checks in on the attendant as well as your parent.

    Can you help if my parent has memory problems?

    Yes. We arrange a cognitive screening with a geriatrician or memory clinic, explain the diagnosis in plain words, and train family members and attendants in dementia care. Early diagnosis opens up more options.

    What does it cost?

    Your first care forecast and family call are free for everyone. Ongoing care management has a monthly fee on a sliding scale linked to household income, and elders with no family support or limited means pay nothing. The fees cover our costs. As a Section 8 company, Sannishtha Foundation cannot pay profits or dividends to anyone, so any surplus goes back into free care for elders who cannot pay.

    Why is a not-for-profit doing this?

    Families tell us they no longer trust advice from agencies and senior living operators who earn a commission on every placement. We take no commissions or referral fees from anyone we recommend, so our only interest is what is right for your parent.

    Can I get a tax deduction on my donation?

    Donations to Sannishtha Foundation are eligible for deduction under the Income Tax Act, and we send a receipt and certificate for every gift. Whether you can claim it depends on the tax regime you file under, so check with your tax adviser.
